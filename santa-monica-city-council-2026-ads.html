Executive Summary

No one in the Santa Monica City Council race has launched a new Meta ad in 24 days. The last new creative in this contest went live on August 21, 2026 — seven weeks before ballots go out for the November 3 general election. Every advertiser found in this race has gone quiet.

Across the full cycle, four pages account for all disclosed Meta political ad activity touching this race: Doug Trussler for Santa Monica City Council, Eli Gill for Santa Monica City Council, Santa Monica Neighbors — an issue-advocacy page with no committee filing attached — and L.A. Local Network, a new local-news page running paid content promotion. Combined, they account for $7.5K–$16.7K behind 89 ads and roughly 875K–1.08M impressions.

Doug Trussler remains the dominant advertiser at $6,300–$10,858 across 42 ads — roughly 65–84% of all disclosed spend in the race — concentrated in two waves on July 8 and August 11. Santa Monica Neighbors ran 41 ads for far less money ($600–$4,659), entirely in April, well before candidate filing opened. Eli Gill has run 5 low-spend ads. Incumbents Caroline Torosis and Lana Negrete, along with every local labor and advocacy committee checked, still show zero Meta ad activity this cycle.

The finding is the silence

A municipal race with three at-large seats, a contested policy environment, and a November 3 election date would normally see paid activity building through September. Instead, the last new ad in this contest launched August 21 — two weeks after the August 7 filing deadline, and the race has been dark on Meta ever since.

Three readings are consistent with this pattern, and the Ad Library alone cannot distinguish among them: campaigns are holding budget for an October push timed to vote-by-mail (California ballots begin going out roughly October 5); campaigns are spending elsewhere — mail, digital outside Meta, canvass — which the archive cannot see; or the field is simply under-resourced on paid media this cycle. Watch the first week of October: if paid activity does not resume then, the third reading gains weight.

Context on the "$35 million deficit" claim

Trussler's highest-spending August creative is built around Santa Monica's "$35 million deficit." The city's own reporting describes a different trajectory: Finance Director Oscar Santiago told council members that the current fiscal year is projected to close with an $8.95 million surplus, reversing the $29.6 million structural deficit projected in October 2025 when the Realignment Plan was adopted, with the general fund now projected to run surpluses through fiscal year 2030 (Santa Monica Daily Press).

The ad does not specify which fiscal year, fund, or scenario its $35 million figure describes, and the Ad Library does not carry that detail. Both figures are reported here as published; they may refer to different budget years or measures.

What zero means — and doesn't

Absence from the Meta political archive is not absence from the race. It means only that no ad with a "paid for by" disclaimer was filed and delivered. Labor committees in particular often concentrate spending in mail and member-to-member communication, which never reaches this archive. The most defensible reading of the table above is that the institutional actors in Santa Monica politics have not chosen Meta as a persuasion channel this cycle — not that they are inactive.

Methodology & Limitations

Data source. Meta Ad Library API, queried September 14, 2026. Window: January 1 – September 14, 2026. Country filter: United States; region filter: California. Advertisers were located via candidate-name and page-name keyword searches, plus a broad "Santa Monica" and "Santa Monica City Council" keyword scan across California, then resolved to Page IDs before any page-scoped query. This edition supersedes the September 4, 2026 edition; section 01 documents what changed between the two pulls.

Advertisers found and excluded. Tom Shadrach for City Council (Page ID 118238817535406, 43 ads) surfaced in keyword scans but is a Cape Coral, Florida council candidate — excluded as out of geography. Southern California Association of NonProfit Housing, ClientEarth USA, and Union of Concerned Scientists matched the California regional filter but run national issue campaigns unrelated to this race — excluded as out of scope. L.A. Local Network is included, but only its single Santa Monica ad counts toward report totals; its five unrelated ads are excluded.

Known limitations.

  • Spend is a range, never a number. Meta reports bands (e.g., $100–$199). Every headline figure in this report is stated as its full disclosed range. Midpoints appear only in the monthly chart, which is labeled as estimated.
  • Monthly attribution is approximate. Meta reports lifetime spend per ad, not spend per month. The monthly chart attributes each ad's spend to the month it began delivering, so an ad running July 8 through August 11 is counted entirely in July.
  • The archive captures only disclaimer-filed ads. Mail, yard signs, earned media, unpaid social posts, and any ad run without a "paid for by" disclaimer are invisible here. A candidate with zero Meta ads may still be spending heavily through other channels. The 24-day blackout is a Meta-specific finding, not evidence that campaigns have stopped spending.
  • Keyword coverage is not exhaustive. A committee whose ad text and page name never contain a candidate's name, "Santa Monica," or "City Council" could be missed by this search approach.
  • Delivery is not targeting. This report makes no claims about whom advertisers targeted. Ad Library data reports where ads were delivered, which reflects Meta's optimization as much as any advertiser selection.
  • This report covers Meta ad spend only. It does not incorporate campaign finance filings, fundraising totals, or other paid media, which were out of scope for this pull.
