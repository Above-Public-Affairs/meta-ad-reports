Executive Summary

As of September 4, 2026, three Facebook/Instagram pages account for all disclosed Meta political ad activity found in the Santa Monica City Council race: Doug Trussler for Santa Monica City Council, Eli Gill for Santa Monica City Council, and Santa Monica Neighbors — an issue-advocacy page with no candidate committee attached. Combined, they've put $5.9K–$15.0K behind 88 ads and roughly 725K–897K impressions, running in three distinct, non-overlapping windows between April 8 and August 21, 2026.

Doug Trussler is the dominant advertiser by spend — 42 ads and $4,900–$9,458, more than half the combined total — running Jul 8–Aug 11 on downtown decline, crime/homelessness, and the city's budget deficit. Santa Monica Neighbors ran nearly as many ads (41) for far less money ($600–$4,659), entirely in a three-week window in April, well before candidate filing closed. Eli Gill has run 5 low-spend ads from late May through August.

No other candidate or organization active in this race — including incumbents Caroline Torosis and Lana Negrete — has any Meta ad activity on record for this cycle as of this report's retrieval date.

Methodology & Limitations

Data source. Meta Ad Library API, queried September 4, 2026. Window: January 1 – September 4, 2026. Country filter: United States; region filter: California. Advertisers were located via candidate-name and page-name keyword searches, plus a broad "Santa Monica City Council" keyword scan across California, then resolved to Page IDs before any page-scoped query.

Candidates and organizations checked with no 2026 Meta ad activity found. Mayor Caroline Torosis, Lana Negrete, Ericka Lesley, Phil Brock, Danny Ivanov, Brett Morrow, Ashley Oelsen, Derrick Townsend, Ryan Blomberg, Lindsay Davis, and Wade Kelley were each searched directly by name; Santa Monicans for Renters' Rights (SMRR), the Santa Monica Police Officers Association, Santa Monica Fire Fighters IAFF Local 1109, and Santa Monica Forward were checked directly by Page ID. None returned any ad delivered in 2026 as of this report's retrieval date. This does not mean these candidates or groups aren't campaigning — only that no Meta-disclosed political ad from them was found.

Known limitations.

  • Spend is a range, never a number. Meta reports bands (e.g., $100–$199). Every figure in this report is stated as its full disclosed range; no midpoint estimates are presented as totals.
  • The archive captures only disclaimer-filed ads. Mail, yard signs, earned media, unpaid social posts, and any ad run without a "paid for by" disclaimer are invisible here. A candidate with zero Meta ads may still be spending heavily through other channels.
  • Keyword coverage is not exhaustive. A committee whose ad text and page name never contain the candidate's name, "Santa Monica," or "City Council" could be missed by this search approach.
  • "Still running" ads are ongoing. Trussler's Aug 11 budget-deficit ad and Gill's Aug 21 platform ad remained active as of retrieval; their final spend and impression totals will be higher than reported here.
  • This report covers Meta ad spend only. It does not incorporate campaign finance filings, fundraising totals, or other paid media, which were out of scope for this pull.
