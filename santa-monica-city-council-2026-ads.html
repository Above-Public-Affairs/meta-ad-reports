Executive Summary

With 50 days to the November 3 election, the Santa Monica City Council race is being contested on Meta almost entirely by one candidate, at a total disclosed spend that would be unremarkable for a single state legislative district. Four pages account for all Meta political ad activity touching this race, together disclosing $7,500–$16,810 across 90 ads and roughly 875K–1.08M impressions.

Doug Trussler is the dominant advertiser at $6,300–$10,858 across 42 ads — between 65% and 84% of all disclosed spend in the race — launched in two waves on July 8 and August 11. Eli Gill is the only candidate who has put new creative into market recently, launching a new ad on September 14, and now totals 6 ads at $600–$1,194. Santa Monica Neighbors, an issue-advocacy page, ran a concentrated 41-ad burst in April on the Ocean Avenue housing siting process and Mayor Torosis's outside income, and has not advertised since May 1. L.A. Local Network, a local-news page, promoted one story about a council zoning vote.

The more striking finding is who is absent. Santa Monicans for Renters' Rights, the police and firefighter associations, Santa Monica Forward, the city employees' PAC, and incumbents Caroline Torosis and Lana Negrete have run zero Meta political ads in 2026. The institutions that have historically organized Santa Monica elections are not using this channel at all.

Reading the spend increases correctly

Trussler's disclosed spend rose roughly $1,400 between the two pulls without a single new ad. That is accrual on ads already in market, not new buying: 20 of his 42 ads were still delivering in September. Meta reports lifetime spend per ad as a widening range while the ad runs, so a static creative set produces a rising total.

The practical implication for anyone tracking this race: ad count and spend move independently here. Watching only the spend line would suggest an accelerating campaign; watching only launch dates would suggest a stalled one. Neither is right on its own.

Context on the "$35 million deficit" claim

Trussler's highest-spending August creative is built around Santa Monica's "$35 million deficit." The city's own reporting describes a different trajectory: Finance Director Oscar Santiago told council members that the current fiscal year is projected to close with an $8.95 million surplus, reversing the $29.6 million structural deficit projected in October 2025 when the Realignment Plan was adopted, with the general fund now projected to run surpluses through fiscal year 2030 (Santa Monica Daily Press).

The ad does not specify which fiscal year, fund, or scenario its $35 million figure describes, and the Ad Library does not carry that detail. Both figures are reported here as published; they may refer to different budget years or measures.

Why this page has no committee filing — and probably doesn't need one

An earlier edition of this report flagged the absence of an FPPC or City Clerk filing for this page. On review of the applicable thresholds, that framing was wrong: on the available evidence this page most likely had no obligation to register at all. Three reasons:

1. The ads are issue advocacy, not express advocacy. California triggers independent-expenditure committee registration for spending that supports or opposes a candidate. Under FPPC Regulation 18225, that means either express words — "vote for," "elect," "defeat" — or, within 60 days of an election, content that taken as a whole unambiguously urges an electoral result. These ads ran April 8 – May 1, roughly six to seven months before the November 3 election, contain no express words of advocacy, and ran before candidate filing had even opened. Torosis was a sitting mayor, not yet a candidate.

2. The spend may never have crossed the threshold. Registration attaches at $1,000 in independent expenditures. Meta discloses this page's total only as the range $600–$4,659, which straddles that line. Thirty-five of its 41 ads sit in the $0–$99 band. The Ad Library cannot resolve whether $1,000 was reached.

3. The "paid for by" label is Meta's rule, not California's. Meta requires authorization and a disclaimer for any ad touching elections, legislation, or social issues — explicitly including issue advocacy that asks for no votes. The presence of a disclaimer therefore says nothing about whether a legal filing was required. Reading a platform label as evidence of a campaign-finance obligation conflates two separate systems.

What remains a fair observation

No founders, staff, officers, or funders are named on the group's website or in its ad disclaimers, and no public reporting identifies them. It is a distinct entity from the city's seven recognized neighborhood associations (Friends of Sunset Park, North of Montana, Ocean Park, Pico, Mid City, Northeast Neighbors, Wilshire Montana), which receive city grant funding and have identifiable leadership.

That opacity is worth noting — someone spent money to shape opinion about a sitting mayor and a housing siting decision, and residents cannot tell who. But it reflects how US disclosure law treats issue advocacy outside the pre-election window, not evidence of evasion or non-compliance. Should this page resume advertising after September 4 — inside the 60-day window — the express-advocacy analysis would change materially.

What zero means — and doesn't

Absence from the Meta political archive is not absence from the race. It means only that no ad with a "paid for by" disclaimer was filed and delivered. Labor committees in particular often concentrate spending in mail and member-to-member communication, which never reaches this archive. The defensible reading is that the institutional actors in Santa Monica politics have not chosen Meta as a persuasion channel this cycle — not that they are inactive.

Methodology & Limitations

Data source. Meta Ad Library API, queried September 14, 2026. Window: January 1 – September 14, 2026. Country filter: United States; region filter: California. Advertisers were located via candidate-name and page-name keyword searches, plus a broad "Santa Monica" and "Santa Monica City Council" keyword scan across California, then resolved to Page IDs before any page-scoped query. Delivery status was confirmed with a separate active-status query.

Advertisers found and excluded. Tom Shadrach for City Council (Page ID 118238817535406, 43 ads) surfaced in keyword scans but is a Cape Coral, Florida council candidate — excluded as out of geography. Southern California Association of NonProfit Housing, ClientEarth USA, and Union of Concerned Scientists matched the California regional filter but run national issue campaigns unrelated to this race — excluded as out of scope. L.A. Local Network is included, but only its single Santa Monica ad counts toward report totals.

Known limitations.

  • Spend is a range, never a number. Meta reports bands (e.g., $100–$199). Every headline figure here is stated as its full disclosed range. Midpoints appear only in the monthly chart, which is labeled as estimated.
  • Launch date is not delivery period. Meta reports lifetime spend per ad, not spend per month, and an ad's disclosed total keeps rising while it runs. An advertiser can show increasing spend with no new creative — as Trussler did between the September 4 and September 14 pulls. Ad counts and spend totals must be read together.
  • The archive captures only disclaimer-filed ads. Mail, yard signs, earned media, unpaid social posts, and any ad run without a "paid for by" disclaimer are invisible here. A candidate with zero Meta ads may still be spending heavily through other channels.
  • A Meta disclaimer is not a campaign-finance filing. Meta requires authorization and a "paid for by" label for any ad touching elections, legislation, or social issues, including pure issue advocacy. Its presence or absence says nothing about obligations under California or Santa Monica law.
  • Keyword coverage is not exhaustive. A committee whose ad text and page name never contain a candidate's name, "Santa Monica," or "City Council" could be missed by this search approach.
  • Delivery is not targeting. This report makes no claims about whom advertisers targeted. Ad Library data reports where ads were delivered, which reflects Meta's optimization as much as any advertiser selection.
  • This report covers Meta ad spend only. It does not incorporate campaign finance filings, fundraising totals, or other paid media, which were out of scope for this pull.
