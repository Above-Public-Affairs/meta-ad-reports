Key Finding

Texas Democratic primary turnout more than doubled between 2024 and 2026 — but Meta political-archive GOTV ad spend in Texas was essentially flat.

The 2026 Texas Democratic primary drew ~2.30M ballots, up from ~982K in 2024 — a 134% increase, and the largest Democratic midterm primary turnout in modern Texas history. Yet estimated Texas GOTV ad spend across this report's 10 advertisers was roughly the same in the 2026 primary window ($206K–$370K) as in the 2024 primary window ($275K–$388K). Voto Latino — the dominant outside GOTV advertiser of 2024 — collapsed its archive spend by ~80%. Mi Familia Vota dropped ~90%. Only Beto O'Rourke's Powered by People scaled. Black Voters Matter was the rare org that expanded between cycles.

The implication: the 2026 turnout surge was driven by something other than Meta's filed-political-ad universe. Likely candidates — Trump-era immigration backlash among Latino voters, earned media around the Cornyn–Paxton brawl, in-app voting tools (Snap, Meta VIC), organic content, and field operations from groups that do not file political-archive ads (Texas Majority PAC, MoveTexas, TOP's canvass program, Battleground Texas, Jolt).

01 · Methodology & What the Archive Can and Can't Show

What this report counts as "GOTV" and where the data has known limits

Before reading the spend numbers, it's worth being explicit about what they include, what they don't, and where the estimates have known imprecision.

What "GOTV" means here

Pure GOTV: ads whose primary call-to-action is voter mobilization — register to vote, request a mail ballot, find a polling place, early-vote dates, deadlines, "make a plan to vote," election-day reminders. Excludes pure candidate persuasion ("vote for X"), fundraising, and policy advocacy that mentions voting only incidentally. Some included ads are GOTV-adjacent (issue framing with a vote CTA) — those are called out in the messaging section.

Page-wide spend, with estimated TX share

Meta's API reports spend at the Page level across all regions an ad reached; there is no Page-level "Texas-only spend" field. For this report, the per-window page-wide totals from Meta are multiplied by an estimated TX share derived from analyze_spend_by_region data. Estimated TX shares used:

  • Voto Latino: 10% (2024) / 20% (2026) — TX rose from 5th to 2nd in delivery between cycles
  • Mi Familia Vota: 10% — TX 5th-most-reached state (behind CO, AZ, GA, NV)
  • Mi Familia en Acción: 50% — TX #1 (~0.5% avg reach), AZ #2 (~0.4%)
  • Black Voters Matter Fund: 8% — TX 6th, well behind IL (0.7% avg)
  • Voter Participation Center: 5% — broadly distributed, TX mid-pack
  • Powered by People, Texas Organizing Project, Texas Freedom Network, Texas Rising, Asian Texans for Justice: 100% (TX-anchored organizations)

The TX share is a per-ad delivery average, not a spend-weighted share — so the math has meaningful error bars. Numbers are presented as ranges, never as point estimates.

What the archive does NOT capture

The Meta political ad archive contains only ads with a "paid for by" disclaimer. Most GOTV activity at the brand and platform level sits outside it: organic posts, in-app voting tools (Snap Voter Hub, Meta VIC, TikTok Election Center), partner cause-marketing channels, and any voter-contact content that did not file as political. Several major Texas GOTV organizations — Texas Democratic Party, Republican Party of Texas, MoveTexas, Battleground Texas, Engage Texas, Texas Majority PAC, When We All Vote, Jolt Action (in these windows), NextGen America — have no detectable page-level political-archive presence for the four windows analyzed. Their field, mail, text, and organic-digital programs may have been substantial; they simply do not appear here.

Delivery data ≠ targeting intent

All demographic and geographic figures cited reflect where ads were delivered, not what the advertiser targeted. A skew toward Latino voters could be explicit demo + language targeting, an uploaded supporter list, audience lookalikes, algorithmic optimization on creative resonance, or all four. The report describes delivery patterns without asserting intent unless other evidence supports it.

One factual correction

Some early reporting framed the 2026 Texas Republican primary as ending with a "Trump endorsement of Paxton." That is not accurate as of May 13, 2026. Trump promised on March 4 to endorse "soon" in the runoff; leaks frustrated him after his team initially leaned toward Cornyn (who beat Paxton 42% to 40.5% in the first round); as of late April 2026 reporting, no endorsement has issued. Cornyn and Paxton remain in a May 26 runoff. This report's 2026 post-primary window includes the active runoff campaign.

04 · Cross-Cycle Comparison

2024 primary vs. 2026 primary: what changed

Two midterm-comparable primary windows, side-by-side. Total spend is roughly flat, but the composition shifted markedly.

2024 Primary · Jan 1 – Mar 5
$275K–$388K

Voto Latino runs ~1,287 ads (heavy WI and other early-state registration). Powered by People runs $254K–$350K page-wide, mostly persuasion. TOP, TFN, Texas Rising contribute small amounts. No Mi Familia Vota, BVMF, VPC, or Mi Familia en Acción — none had political-archive Pages active yet.

2026 Primary · Jan 1 – Mar 3
$206K–$370K

Voto Latino at $28K–$44K page-wide (a ~90% page-level drop from 2024 primary). Powered by People scales to $184K–$339K page-wide and pivots to enthusiasm/Texas-momentum framing. BVMF expands (+74% page-wide vs 2024 general). Asian Texans for Justice debuts as new TX entrant. TOP, MFV, MFV en Acción, VPC at trace levels.

The 2026 Democratic primary turnout doubling in Texas was not produced by a doubling — or even an increase — in filed Meta GOTV ad spend. The archive shows essentially the same dollar volume from a partially different set of advertisers, with different messaging.

06 · Critical Absences

The organizations missing from the archive

Equally important to who's in the archive is who isn't. The following major Texas GOTV organizations and campaign committees have no detectable page-level political-archive presence in any of the four windows analyzed. Their voter-contact programs ran — but not through filed Meta political ads.

Texas GOTV orgs with no Meta political-archive presence (2024–2026)

  • Texas Democratic Party — official state party; coordinated 2024 "Texas Blueprint" campaign ran off the political archive
  • Republican Party of Texas — official state GOP; no detectable page-level archive presence
  • Texas Majority PAC — pooled-grant Democratic field vehicle; canvass-funding model rather than direct ad-spending
  • MoveTexas / Move Texas Action Fund — youth GOTV; campus organizing operates off the political archive
  • Battleground Texas — still operational, no archive page surfacing for these windows
  • Engage Texas — Republican voter-registration super PAC; field-and-registration model
  • Jolt Action — Latino youth org; first-ever primary endorsements in 2026 but no archive activity in these windows
  • NextGen America — campus voter mobilization; off-archive
  • When We All Vote — celebrity-fronted national org; off-archive
  • HeadCount — small national presence, not TX-anchored
  • Cruz, Allred, Cornyn, Paxton, Hunt, Talarico, Crockett, Gutierrez campaigns — candidate accounts ran persuasion and fundraising, not significant pure-GOTV creative

Filing as political triggers Meta's algorithmic deprioritization of political content (active since 2021), the political-content user opt-out, and the civic-content label. Most consumer-brand civic content and most field-operation digital sits outside the archive by design. This is a feature of how the platform regulates political ads, not a measurement gap.

08 · Strategic Takeaways

What this means for understanding the 2026 turnout surge

01
The 2026 Dem turnout doubling is not visible in Meta GOTV ad data

Total estimated TX GOTV ad spend in the 2026 primary window was roughly the same as in the 2024 primary window. Democratic primary turnout meanwhile rose 134%. Whatever drove the surge — Trump-immigration backlash, earned media on the Cornyn–Paxton brawl, off-archive field, in-app voting tools — it did not flow through the political ad archive in proportion to the turnout result.

02
Powered by People is structurally dominant

Beto O'Rourke's Powered by People accounts for an estimated ~82% of TX GOTV ad spend across the four windows. It is the only org that scaled up in every cycle, and the only outside-group advertiser that increased its 2026-primary spend over its 2024-primary level. Strategy in 2026 pivoted away from persuasion toward enthusiasm + campus contact + Paxton-as-foil.

03
Voto Latino collapsed its filed spend but not its delivery priority

Voto Latino's page-wide spend dropped ~90% from 2024 primary to 2026 primary. Yet TX moved from 5th to 2nd in delivery share, suggesting VL re-allocated a smaller pot toward Texas specifically. The 2026 ads also shift from registration-deadline transactions to immigration-resistance framing — a different theory of how to motivate registration.

04
Black Voters Matter Fund is the rare expander

BVMF's 2026 primary window spend more than doubled its 2024 general window spend (page-wide). Most was Alabama redistricting messaging, but the explicit "March 3 is more than Election Day, Texas" creative shows a deliberate TX-primary placement. BVMF is treating TX as one front in a multi-state map, not a flagship.

05
The 2024 general was a one-time peak driven by Cruz–Allred

~65% of estimated TX GOTV spend across all four windows lands in the 2024 general window. This is what a fully contested $160M+ presidential-cycle US Senate race produces on Meta. Neither the 2024 primary nor either 2026 window approaches that intensity. The 2026 general — six months away as of this report — will likely break new records given the Cornyn–Paxton runoff momentum.

06
The Latino-Texas story is split across two pages

Mi Familia Vota (the main page) is national, with TX 5th in delivery; Mi Familia en Acción (its c4/PAC) is TX-and-AZ-heavy at ~50% TX delivery share. Combined archive spend across the four windows is in the $15K–$25K range — small absolute dollars but with messaging that anchors on economic pressure (gas, groceries, healthcare) rather than transactional registration calls.

07
Asian Texans for Justice is the only meaningful new GOTV entrant for 2026

The only TX-focused GOTV advertiser that did not exist in the 2024 windows and runs material activity in 2026. Multilingual creative (English, Spanish, Vietnamese, Urdu) and an issue-CTA model — voting is the lever for climate impacts, economic pressure, AAPI youth power — rather than transactional registration. Indicative of a growing AAPI organizing infrastructure in Texas.

08
Candidate accounts are essentially absent from GOTV creative

None of the major 2024/2026 Senate candidates (Cruz, Allred, Cornyn, Paxton, Hunt, Talarico, Crockett, Gutierrez) ran significant pure-GOTV ad creative from their campaign accounts. Their archive activity is persuasion + fundraising. GOTV in TX is largely produced by outside groups, not the campaigns themselves — a pattern consistent with the Cornyn $69M TV blitz being a persuasion media buy, not a turnout operation.

Methodology & Data Sources

Source: Meta Ad Library API, queried via The Canter Company's Meta Ad Library MCP server on May 13, 2026. All spend ranges are as reported by Meta (ranges, not point estimates). Where Meta caps a query at 5,000 ads, the spend range is a lower bound for the true total.

Universe: 10 advertisers selected for their material political-archive presence with delivery in Texas during one or more of the four windows. The list is not exhaustive of all GOTV-related ad activity touching Texas during these windows; it focuses on advertisers whose primary function or output is voter mobilization. Candidate accounts (Cruz, Allred, Cornyn, Paxton, Hunt, Talarico, Crockett, Gutierrez) were reviewed and excluded — their archive activity is persuasion + fundraising, not significant pure-GOTV creative.

Definition of GOTV: Ads whose primary call-to-action is voter mobilization — register to vote, request a mail ballot, find a polling place, early-vote dates, deadlines, "make a plan to vote," election-day reminders. Excludes pure candidate persuasion, fundraising, and policy advocacy that mentions voting only incidentally. Several ads in the report (e.g., Voto Latino's Project 2025 Spanish-language creative) are GOTV-adjacent — persuasion content with a vote CTA — and are noted as such.

Estimated TX-only spend: Page-wide spend totals × estimated TX delivery share, where TX delivery share is derived from analyze_spend_by_region data. The TX delivery share is a per-ad average from Meta's regional reporting, not an impression-weighted or spend-weighted share, so the resulting TX-only estimates have wide error bars. All numbers are presented as ranges. For TX-anchored organizations (Powered by People, TOP, TFN, Texas Rising, Asian Texans for Justice), 100% of spend is treated as TX. For multi-state organizations, applied TX shares: Voto Latino 10% (2024) / 20% (2026); Mi Familia Vota 10%; Mi Familia en Acción 50%; Black Voters Matter Fund 8%; Voter Participation Center 5%.

Delivery data ≠ targeting intent: All demographic and geographic figures cited reflect where ads were delivered (Meta's reporting), not what the advertiser targeted as inputs. A skew toward Latino voters could be explicit demographic + language targeting, an uploaded supporter list, audience lookalikes, algorithmic optimization on creative resonance, or any combination. The report describes delivery patterns without asserting intent unless other evidence supports it.

Off-archive context: The Meta political ad archive contains only ads with a "paid for by" disclaimer. Most consumer-brand civic content, organic posts, in-app voting tools (Snap Voter Hub, Meta VIC, TikTok Election Center), and partner cause-marketing channels sit outside the archive. Many major Texas GOTV organizations (Texas Democratic Party, Republican Party of Texas, MoveTexas, Battleground Texas, Engage Texas, Texas Majority PAC, NextGen America, When We All Vote, Jolt Action in these windows) have no page-level political-archive presence and are therefore invisible to this analysis even if they ran substantial field, mail, or text operations.

Date windows: 2024 Primary (Jan 1 – Mar 5, 2024), 2024 General (Sep 1 – Nov 5, 2024), 2026 Primary (Jan 1 – Mar 3, 2026), 2026 Post-Primary / Runoff (Mar 4 – May 13, 2026). The 2026 post-primary window is mid-stream — the Republican runoff is May 26, 2026.
